Internal Research

RySS has developed an internal research ecosystem to build the scientific foundation for Andhra Pradesh Community Managed Natural Farming (APCNF), generating evidence directly from farmers' fields. Its research responds to questions and observations emerging from farmers and evaluates natural farming against conventional chemical farming through systematic, long-term trials. The research covers soil health, biodiversity, pest management, bio-inputs, Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing (PMDS), 365 Days Green Cover, water-use efficiency, and resilience to extreme weather events. Studies have documented reduced water requirements, lower crop damage, improved biodiversity, healthier soils and crops, and stronger biological indicators. A continuous pipeline of field and laboratory research further examines bio-input optimization, seed diversity, intercropping, and soil microbial health. Importantly, research findings are converted into farming protocols, training resources, and scaling strategies, enabling evidence from farmers' fields to inform practice and policy.

Community-Managed Natural Farming in Andhra Pradesh via Internal Research Initiatives

Initially, natural farming necessitated thorough investigation, and agricultural research institutions were skeptical regarding its advantages for farmers and the accompanying evidence. Nevertheless, RySS possessed an internal team bolstered by a robust and adaptive research initiative that guarantees the feasibility, measurability, and continual advancement of Andhra Pradesh Community Managed Natural Farming (APCNF) by generating evidence directly from farmers' fields via its Science Team.

Research Rooted in Farmers' Realities:

The study's internal research at APCNF revealed that a significant number of inquiries originated from farmers. In the subsequent phase, these observations are transformed into organized research that may be utilized to evaluate, authenticate, and enhance APCNF procedures in contexts that reflect real-world conditions.

What the RySS in-house research team does: The research team compares APCNF methods to conventional chemical farming practices in controlled, long-term trials. Summary of study spanning a decade on the positive impacts of natural agricultural practices during times of extreme weather, including floods, cyclones, and winds. In addition to providing feedback to farmers and conducting comparative evaluations of soil health, biodiversity, and pest management, research focuses on standardizing bio-input formulations, Pre-Monsoon Dry Sowing (PMDS), 365 Days Green Cover advantages, standardizing spray intervals, and standardizing bio-inputs in APCNF through systematic trials.

Generating Evidence That Matters: Internal studies have highlighted reductions in water use, improved climate resilience, stronger biodiversity outcomes, and healthier soils and crops. Research has documented fewer irrigation requirements, lower crop damage during extreme weather events, increased bird populations, and improved biological indicators such as earthworm activity and plant Brix readings.

A Continuous Research Pipeline: RySS maintains an ongoing programme of field and laboratory research, including studies on bio-input optimization, seed diversity, intercropping, water-use efficiency, and soil microbial analysis.

Turning Research into Action: Findings are translated into standardized farming protocols, farmer training resources, and scaling strategies. This allows evidence generated in the field to directly inform practice and policy.

Building an Evidence-Based Future for Natural Farming: By combining farmer-led innovation with scientific rigor, RySS has created a living research ecosystem that helps natural farming evolve, adapt, and demonstrate measurable outcomes at scale.
